# Catalog and build order

> Which components exist, in what order they are built, and what has been
> ruled out.
> **This is not a foundation.** Foundations are permanent rules; this is a
> living list that changes weekly. They are kept apart so the foundations do
> not look less stable than they are.

**Status:** a chosen list, not a queue · **Date:** 2026-09-07

---

## 1. A warning about this list

The list below is a **forecast**, based on what nearly every management
application needs. It is not a commitment, and it is not a queue that has to be
worked through in order.

Which of these get built, and in what order, is a deliberate decision
([decision 0008](./decisions/0008-the-rule-of-two-splits.md)). Real screens
change that decision when they exist — that is the point of having a consumer —
but their absence does not block it.

## 2. How a component gets in

1. It is a **component**, not a prop on one that already exists. If the need
   can be met by an existing component, that is the cheaper answer — and the
   prop it would take faces the stricter half of P5
2. It passes the **thirteen checkboxes** of the entry gate
   ([doc 01](./foundations/01-principles.md)). Thirteen of thirteen, not twelve
3. It breaks no non-goal

No component enters half-built. "Since we're already here" is still the reason
things rot — it just rots through props rather than through the catalog.

## 3. How the order is decided

Three criteria, in strict priority:

**1. Technical dependency.** Overrides everything else. You cannot build a
select without having the field and the layers first.

**2. Real demand, when it exists.** Within what criterion 1 permits, real
screens dictate the order over this list. With no consumer yet, that criterion
is quiet and the order falls to criteria 1 and 3 — which is why the bottlenecks
and the risky piece come first.

**3. Risk, once.** A difficult component is built **early**, even if nobody has
asked for it: if the composition model does not hold, it is far better to find
out with five components built than with twenty.

## 4. The two bottlenecks

**`Field` and the layer base.** More than twenty components depend on them, and
in practice they are irreversible: redesigning them later forces a change to
everything.

They deserve more care than anything else, and it is no coincidence that the
day of experiments with the headless base consists of exactly a dialog with a
dropdown inside and a field with a label, description and error. That day
validates both.

## 6. The risk component

**`ComboBox`, built in the early phase**, even though no project has asked for
it yet.

Reason: it stresses the entire architecture in a single component — field,
layers, keyboard, filtering, locale, long lists — and it is small enough to
redo if it comes out wrong. If the composition model does not hold up to
`ComboBox`, it will not hold up to the table either, and it is far better to
know that then.

The table pieces come afterwards, with the architecture already validated.

A consumer who needs that is building a second notification system beside this one, and the region is already rendering the answer                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| A corner prop on `ToastRegion`                      | Pending a case      | Bottom, inline end, which flips in Arabic. The top of a management screen is where the application's own furniture lives, and a stack that covers it hides the thing somebody was about to press. Four corners would arrive with four sets of entry directions to keep agreeing with them                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| A polite (non-interrupting) `Toast`                 | **Never**           | The base gives the content `role="alert"`, which interrupts. A notice that waited for a pause in the reading would arrive after the thing it describes had scrolled away, and choosing between the two per notice is a decision no consumer has the context to make                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| A `timeout` prop on a notice                        | **Never**           | One decision for the library, from the notice itself (doc 09 §4.1): six seconds ordinarily, ten with an action to take, and a `danger` notice does not leave at all. Per-instance timings are what make two screens in the same application feel like two applications                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| Stacking `Toast` notices without a limit            | **Never**           | Three, newest first, and the rest wait with their full time — measured, a hidden notice does not spend its clock. A stack that reaches the middle of the screen has stopped being a notification and started being a dialog nobody opened                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| A `size` scale on `Popover`                         | **Never**           | A popover is anchored to a control and sized by what is in it, up to `--container-medium`. A scale of widths would be inventing a decision nobody has to make, and the height is not ours at all — the base measures the room between the trigger and the edge of the window and writes the ceiling itself                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| `offset`, `containerPadding` or `shouldFlip` props  | **Never**           | The distance from a trigger is spacing, from a token, and one value for the library (doc 02 §3.3, `LAYER_OFFSET`). Whether to reposition a layer that will not fit is not a preference — the answer is always yes, and the base already does it correctly                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| A `Popover` that matches its trigger's width        | Pending a case      | The base publishes `--trigger-width`, so it is one declaration away, and a `Select` will want it. Rule 8: nothing needs it today                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| Container queries inside a `Popover`                | **Never**           | Not a decision so much as a measured impossibility, and it is here because it looks like an omission. A popover's width comes from its contents, and `container-type: inline-size` computes an element's inline size as though it had no contents — so declaring one collapses the panel to its borders, measured at 2px. `width: max-content` does not rescue it either. Doc 04 §4.3 carries the law and decision 0010 the cost it was predicted from                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| `Drawer` as a `variant` on `Dialog`                 | **Never**           | They share the scrim, the panel and the whole sheet, and `src/internal/Layer` is where that lives. What they do not share is the two things a consumer sets: a `side` means nothing on a centred dialog, and `size` would measure a different axis depending on `side` — one component whose props change meaning according to another prop is exactly non-goal 4, and it is the same argument that rejected a multiple-value `NumberField`. The duplication a reader worries about is six lines of placement; the rest is shared already                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |

## 8. How this is maintained

- **Every finished component** updates its status here, not somewhere else.
- **Every decision to build something** updates this list before the work
  starts, not after. The list is the record of what was chosen.
- **Every rejection** goes in section 7 with its reason, even when it seems
  obvious at the time.
- This document is **not** moved into the foundations: it lives apart and
  changes often.
